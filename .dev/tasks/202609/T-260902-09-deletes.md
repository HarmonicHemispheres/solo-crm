## Why

Reported: *"we cant delete engagements, companys or people or offerings. we
need a way to do this."*

Correct, and worse than it sounds. `companies:delete`, `people:delete` and
`engagements:delete` had existed since T-260828-26 and **nothing in the
renderer ever called them**. Offerings had no delete channel at all. And even
from main the delete refused whenever anything referenced the row, with
messages naming blockers the app gave the operator no way to clear —
activity above all, which is append-only and has no delete of its own.

## Story

As the operator, I can remove a record I do not want, having been told
exactly what else that removes.

## Constraints

- The refusing delete stays the default: nothing cascades by accident.
- The confirmation's counts must be the delete's counts.
- ADR-011's attachments and `company_images` cascade by trigger and by
  foreign key; neither gets a second copy of that rule.

## Acceptance

- [x] All four entities can be deleted from the UI.
- [x] The dialog lists what would be deleted and, separately, what would be
      kept and unlinked.
- [x] Nothing is deleted until the second, explicit confirmation.
- [x] After a cascade, `foreign_key_check` is empty.
- [x] Drive the real app: delete a company with engagements attached.

## Outcome

**Changed:** new `repositories/cascade.ts` (the plans, the preview and the
cascade), `shared/deletion.ts` (the wire shape), and
`components/primitives/ConfirmDelete.tsx` (one dialog for all four). Each
`deleteX` takes `cascade = false`; each gains a `<entity>DeleteImpact`
reader; `deleteOffering` is new. Six channels added. Delete affordances on
both detail headers and on the engagement and offering rows.

**Departed from scope:** Three, all recorded in ADR-017.

`Button` gains a `danger` variant. Its union was closed deliberately
(T-260901-01) and the comment said opening it would be a scope change; this
is that change, made because `.claude/rules/ui-design.md` already names
destructive confirmation as a place a text label is required, and a
delete-everything button that looks like Cancel is the one mistake here that
cannot be undone. `Button.test.tsx`'s "does not compile with a variant the
mockup does not have" used `'danger'` as its counter-example and now uses
`'subtle'` — the property it tests is unchanged.

`Offerings.tsx` said "Archive, never delete" in its header and its view
description. Both are rewritten: archiving and deleting are different
questions, and the view now offers both.

`archiveOffering` keeps its meaning entirely. Deleting an offering never
deletes an engagement sold from it — it unlinks and keeps
`agreed_rate_cents`.

**Not verified:** Nothing, and two bugs were caught by verifying rather than
by reasoning:

The preview *over-counted*. A company's activity was reached by two steps
(through its engagements, and directly) and counted twice while being deleted
once. `cascade.test.ts` compares the promised total against rows actually
removed, which is what found it; the three tables that can be reached both
ways are now one step with an `OR`.

The confirm button read **"Delete all 4"** over a list of seven rows — it
counted *entries*, not rows. Found by driving the real app. Understating the
count on the button that does the deleting is the exact failure the dialog
exists to prevent, so it is now pinned by a test with those numbers in it.

**Elapsed:** ~2 hours.
